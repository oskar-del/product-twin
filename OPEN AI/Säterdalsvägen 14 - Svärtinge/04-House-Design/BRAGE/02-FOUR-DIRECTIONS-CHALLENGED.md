# 02 · Diverge — the Brain's four, challenged, plus two it missed

The Brain sketched four directions and recommended "A with B's heart." Good instinct, thin argument. Below each is re-derived from the evidence read (sheet 01), stress-tested, and — critically — **the diverge set is completed** with the two evidence factors the Brain never designed against: **evening-sun comfort** and **wind**.

Naming: memorable, Swedish where it helps the resale story.

---

## A · "Långhuset" — Souterräng Longhouse *(Brain's A)*
**Parti:** one long bar along the south frontage, walk-out lower level stepping down toward the lake.
**From the data:** the wide E–W plot genuinely wants a bar; a south bar maximises lake view + winter sun + solar-ready roof plane. Strong.
**Challenge:** the walk-out lower level **assumes a south slope we have not measured** (terrain = 401). On a flat plot the souterräng becomes an expensive dug half-basement that reads "damp" at resale in this market. **A pure straight bar also fails the evening-sun test** — its own south wall shades the terrace after ~18:00 in summer.
**Verdict:** keep the bar spine; *drop the slope dependency*; fix the evening-sun flaw. → feeds the winner.

## B · "Gårdshuset" — Courtyard House *(Brain's B)*
**Parti:** rooms wrap a sheltered inner court.
**From the data:** the *instinct* is right — this climate rewards a sheltered outdoor pocket (the "heart"/vindficka). A court is calm and private from the road.
**Challenge:** a **four-sided court over-encloses** and turns its back on the very thing that sells this plot — the open south lake view — while buying the most expensive thing in a cold climate: **maximum heated perimeter** per m² of house. Wrong economics, wrong view.
**Verdict:** reject as a whole; **graft its heart** — but as a *three-sided* re-entrant pocket open to the S/SW view, not a closed court.

## C · "Två Volymer" — Two Volumes *(Brain's C)*
**Parti:** house and a second volume (garage/annex) as separate massing.
**From the data:** splitting the **garage off as a linked cold volume shortens the heated envelope** (good drift/cost) and a double garage is a hard resale checkbox here.
**Challenge:** as a standalone concept, two freestanding heated volumes double the envelope and the roof-junction detailing — cost + heat-loss for little gain, and "two houses" confuses the resale story.
**Verdict:** reject as a whole; **graft the split garage** as a cold wing that also does climate work (entry court + wind screen).

## D · "Paviljongen" — Pavilion on Plinth *(Brain's D)*
**Parti:** light fragmented pavilions on a raised plinth.
**From the data:** photogenic; a plinth could finesse an unknown slope.
**Challenge:** **fails resale psychology hardest** (sheet 01) — fragmented pavilions read as odd, cold and expensive in conservative Svärtinge, carry the **worst surface-to-volume ratio** for a 58.6°N heating climate, and multiply the open BYA/height risk. This is architecture for a magazine, not for this buyer.
**Verdict:** reject. **Graft one idea only:** a *small detached* pavilion by the water — a **sauna / guest pod** — as an optional phase-2 asset, not the house.

---

## E · "Vinkelhuset mot Glan" — the Angle-House *(BRAGE adds — the evening-sun answer)*
**Why the Brain missed it:** it never put **evening sun** and **wind** on the board, so it never saw that a straight bar is thermally naïve about the hours Swedes actually live outdoors.
**Parti:** an **L / shallow dog-leg**. Main heated bar runs E–W along the south frontage (lake view + winter sun + a south glass face). A **cold service/garage wing steps NORTH off the EAST end**, leaving the **whole west side open** so low WNW evening sun rakes across the south terrace. The **SW gust** is stopped by a *built* glazed windbreak + planting (the vindficka as a screen, not a heated room). Social life sits in the SW view-and-evening-sun corner; sleeping is in the calm east, buffered by the wing.
**From the data:** satisfies every carried evidence factor at once — S view, S winter sun, W evening sun, SW wind shelter, compact heated envelope, double garage, single-level 1.5-plan resale form, and it is **slope-agnostic** (upgrades to a west-end walk-out only *if* DTM confirms fall).
**Verdict:** the synthesis the Brain reached for ("A with B's heart") — but made precise by the two missing factors. **Front-runner.**

## F · "Solfjädern" — the Splayed Fan *(BRAGE adds — the honest alternative)*
**Parti:** a bar whose plan **splays / angles its west social end outward** to chase the WNW evening sun and fan the south view, rather than turning a hard 90° corner.
**From the data:** a softer geometric answer to the same evening-sun problem; can feel more generous from inside.
**Challenge:** non-orthogonal walls raise **build cost and buildability** (every trade prices angles as risk), complicate the prefab **House Heart** and the roof, and add little the L doesn't already deliver — for a conservative-market family house, cleverness the buyer pays for in framing but not at resale.
**Verdict:** honest runner-up; keep as the option if the client wants a signature gesture and accepts the cost. **Graftable idea:** splay *only* the glass of the west view-room, not the structure.
