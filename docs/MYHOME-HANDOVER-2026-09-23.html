MYHOME

Scan your room with your phone. Furnish it from a graph of real products with real dimensions. Order it. We take the commission.

Nothing on the market today lets a person scan their home, say "furnish it like this", and have a system find exact, buyable products and place them. That gap is the product.

Why this, why now

Bliks sits on top of the model vendors' surface, and this month they shipped its core idea natively. Anything built there is one release note from obsolete. MYHOME sits on things a model vendor will never own: a mapped catalogue with live commission links, and the customer's own physical room. Both get stronger with every month of work rather than weaker.

This is deliberately not the plot-to-project platform. Oskar keeps that as consulting for now (one house on one plot at a time). The two products share one engine and one product graph, and nothing else.

The product in one line of user actions

  1. Scan a room on an iPhone (LiDAR / RoomPlan gives walls, doors and windows as geometry).
  2. Furnish: the room compiler selects products from the graph by role and fit, or the user asks in words ("a 3-seat sofa facing the window, oak coffee table, two floor lamps").
  3. See it in 3D, or as a photoreal still, with an honest label on every dimension.
  4. Order: every product carries a channel-tracked affiliate link. Commission 3–15% by merchant.

Steps 2, 3 and 4 exist and run today against a concept house. Step 1 is the new work.

The room compiler prefers source over default and never renders a default as measured. The single biggest lever for MYHOME is moving products up this ladder.

The engine (repo-platform/engine)

  • Scene contract + compiler: rows → placed elements by role (sofa, table, lamp…), dimensional fit, attach points (sofa exposes pillow slots; table exposes centrepiece).
  • Room inside a house: reads a room spec (walls, openings with sill/head heights, finishes) and furnishes it. A LiDAR scan replaces that spec one-for-one.
  • Shoppable surface: click any object → panel with name, brand, price, dimensions + tier chip, BUY. Bundles are self-contained (all geometry inlined, zero runtime fetches).
  • Photoreal still: any composed scene exports to Blender and renders in Cycles (~3 min a frame), stamped VISUALIZATION.
  • Design system: one dark "ink" chrome package (tokens, top bar, side panel, evidence chips). MYHOME will want its own consumer skin on the same components.
  • Gate: 15 test suites, 1 043 checks, including negative controls (every check has been shown to fail on bad input).

The commerce rail

Adtraction channel "Hansson & Hertzell" (content type, approved). Each merchant's product feed is public once approved and pulled by one script into the graph; commission links are carried verbatim, never rewritten. Ten programmes approved, nine still pending (Bosch, Siemens, Elon, Nordic Nest, KitchenTime…). IKEA, Elgiganten and MediaMarkt are on Awin, not Adtraction — a second network signup, not done.

The gap, honestly

  1. Scan ingest. RoomPlan (ARKit) exports a USDZ / JSON with walls, doors, windows, and detected furniture boxes. Convert that into the room spec the compiler already reads. This is the whole new piece, and it is mostly a format adapter plus a native iOS shell.
  2. Consumer front end. Today's surfaces are dark, dense, and made for us. MYHOME needs a phone-first skin on the same engine.
  3. Geometry quality. 3 936 proxies place correctly but look like blocks. The still-render path is what makes it look like a shop; native manufacturer meshes (8 today) are the long-term answer, and licensing them back to manufacturers is a revenue line of its own.
  4. Placeability. 6 390 products with a fully stated size is thin. Mining merchant pages for the height axis, and asking Newport for their spec sheets directly, moves the number fastest.
  5. Language in. "Furnish it like this" needs an intent → room-programme step in front of the compiler. The compiler's role selection is already deterministic, so the model only has to produce a programme, never a product.

How we work, in 21 rules

Earned the hard way over three weeks; each one caught a real defect. The short list that matters most for a consumer product:

01
Computed beats typed. A figure someone typed is a claim; a figure the build derives is evidence.
05
A missing height may be a category default, labelled as such, shown as indicative, never quoted as a product fact.
09
Verify what a label tested, not the number. "Placeable" once silently included invented envelopes.
10
Gate the artefact, not the tool. Two committed renders shipped without their label because the exporter was fixed after they were made.
15
Assert the contract, not the snapshot. A gate that breaks when a supplier ships a fix trains people to ignore it.
16
Every fetch from a static host carries a content hash; every page exposes a counts probe. A stale page renders perfectly and lies.
21
A check never shown to fail on a known-bad input is not evidence. Three checks in two days passed by agreeing with the bug.

First four weeks

Read this, clone the two repos, run npm run engine:gate in repo-platform and open dist/twins/rooms.html. Agree the scan→room-spec adapter contract (a JSON schema, one page).
Week 2
iOS shell with RoomPlan export → adapter → room spec. Prove one real scanned room compiles through the existing pipeline with Newport furniture placed.

Ten friends scan ten rooms. Measure: rooms that compile, products placed, BUY clicks. That number decides the next month.
