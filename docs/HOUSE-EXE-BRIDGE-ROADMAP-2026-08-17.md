# House.exe bridge roadmap · 2026-08-17

## Product thesis

The project is not a furniture viewer, a prefab catalogue or a 3D-printing company in isolation. It is a spatial compiler that turns intent into a continuously traceable physical result.

`PLOT / HOUSE / ROOM / PRODUCT → DESIGN STATE → TECHNICAL CHECKS → AVATARS + QUANTITIES → OFFERS / RFQs → ASSEMBLY → AS-BUILT MEMORY`

Plot-first, house-first, room-first and product-first are different entrances to the same object graph. A decision made at one level should propagate to the others rather than create a disconnected model.

## What the Room Lab proves now

The public Room Lab is the first interior slice of this compiler:

- eight separate, product-bound residential avatars coexist in one verified-scale room;
- every avatar remains removable and inspectable rather than being baked into a render;
- exact identity, geometry fidelity and commercial state are disclosed separately;
- furniture can become a constraint-bearing input to room design rather than decoration added at the end.
- selected avatars now move in 100 mm increments, rotate in 15° increments and report G2 room-boundary, overlap and nearest-gap evidence from their verified planning envelopes.

It does not yet prove the complete loop. Room budget, client-approved substitution, live offer refresh and a first G3 hero remain the next interior gates. The current clearance feedback is a G2 planning check, not an engineering certificate, and the current assets support composition rather than exact visual-likeness claims.

The inventory now has two explicit lanes. **Product Twins** retain exact identity, offers, logistics and the best available product-bound avatar. **Design Assets** may use rights-safe generic geometry such as selected Sweet Home 3D CC0/CC-BY/Free Art models for faster composition. A Design Asset can guide style and fit, but it cannot inherit a retailer identity; the system must find and rank locally procurable Product Twins before purchase.

## The strongest bridge prototype

Build one **Product-to-Building Adapter** before attempting a full manufactured house.

A real G4-capable window is the best candidate because it forces the system to join product identity, geometry, performance and building interfaces. The Product Twin should provide at least:

- width, height, frame depth and weight;
- rough-opening and fixing requirements;
- sill, drainage, flashing and insulation interfaces;
- U-value and relevant performance evidence;
- finish/configuration and current procurement route.

The building side should provide a traceable **window intent** before selecting the product: room and wall, desired view target, seated/standing eye level, preferred time and season of direct light, privacy/noise constraints, overheating/glare limits and facade/structural rules. This enables explainable modes such as morning-light, sunset-view, balanced-daylight and low-cooling-load instead of arbitrary AI window placement.

The compiler should then generate or select:

- the permitted opening/bay configuration;
- a dimensioned adapter/subframe;
- attachment and thermal/drainage interfaces;
- bill-of-material changes and cost delta;
- installation order and verification points.

This is the smallest credible demonstration of mass customization: keep the certified or repeatable building platform stable, and manufacture the interface that lets a real product fit it correctly.

The same adapter pattern later applies to irregular-wall cabinet fillers, replacement windows, stairs, service transitions, façade panels, pergolas and renovation joinery.

## Priority sequence

### Active now · finish Product Twin V0

1. [x] Make the Room Lab manipulable: move, rotate, reset and visible G2 clearance/collision feedback.
2. Add room budget and same-position compare/swap using current offer evidence.
3. Promote one authorized, exact visual furniture avatar to G3 and show it beside disclosed G2 planning proxies.
4. Close one visible end-to-end path from live product to project placement and cart/RFQ.

### Next bridge · connect product to architecture

5. Build a G4 window/system Twin with connection and opening rules.
6. Implement the first Product-to-Building Adapter generator.
7. Let room/product choices update wall bay, quantities, cost and installation constraints.
8. Add an outdoor-room/garden scene to the same compiler: climate, sun, water, mature plant size and procurement become Product Twin constraints, not a separate garden app.

The window bridge should then become the first site-aware optimization proof: compare a small number of valid bay/window schemes against seasonal sun, view cones, privacy, glare/overheating, performance, cost and supply, while preserving the client's explicit preference and explaining every recommendation.

### First complete building proof · after the bridge works

9. Define a compact H30 platform envelope as a test vehicle, not a premature factory commitment.
10. Build its TwinBox as the physical configurator and assembly rehearsal of the same digital object.
11. Model the House Heart/service pod, component identities, red/amber/green work categories and container-by-execution sequence.
12. Compile one frozen design into engineering inputs, cut/print lists, purchase orders, packing order and assembly sequence.

## HouseKit hypothesis to preserve

The promising V1 manufacturing mix is approximately:

- 10% printed intelligence: nodes, adapters and complex interfaces;
- 50% commodity structure/envelope: straight members and repeatable panels;
- 20% prefabricated services: House Heart, bathroom/kitchen/service modules;
- 20% purchased finished products: windows, doors, sanitaryware, furniture and equipment.

Printing should create complexity rather than mass. The platform remains a hypothesis until structural, fire, moisture, acoustic, airtightness, thermal-bridge, durability and assembly testing supports it.

Post-tensioned skeletons, smart structural nodes, integrated air/power/data paths and removable wall cassettes are valuable research directions, but they must not be presented as proven construction systems before engineering and certification.

## TwinBox and lifetime Twin

TwinBox is more than a sales model. The 1:15 or 1:20 plot-and-house twin can:

- let a customer physically configure and price the house;
- teach the real assembly language with matching IDs, colours and keyed connections;
- rehearse construction before full-scale delivery;
- remain after handover as a physical control and diagnostic twin.

The same digital object changes role over time:

1. **TwinBox** — understand, configure and rehearse.
2. **HouseKit** — manufacture and procure the physical building.
3. **Build Agent** — guide, verify and record assembly.
4. **House Brain** — operate, remember and maintain the completed house.
5. **Digital Atelier** — use computationally manufactured interfaces and joinery to make a repeatable platform feel bespoke.

## Commercial flywheel

The public catalogue and Room Lab can create an early business before HouseKit manufacturing exists:

- useful product and comparison pages earn search traffic;
- product pages lead into try-in-room and try-in-house experiences;
- affiliate/cart handoffs, supplier leads and managed procurement monetize the verified loop;
- room and house configurations reveal demand, compatibility failures and missing product data;
- manufacturers can pay for verified high-fidelity Twins and specification-ready catalogue participation.

The flywheel only works if every page provides real identity, fit, evidence or design value. Thin copied catalogue pages would undermine both search quality and trust.

## Decision rule

The immediate question for every new idea is:

> Does it make a real product more identifiable, more faithful, more placeable, more compatible or more procurable inside a real project?

If yes, it can strengthen the active Product Twin loop. If not, preserve it for the House.exe roadmap and keep the V0 build narrow.
